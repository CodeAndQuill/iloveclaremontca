The Prison Library Project

Inside one of the nation's largest books-to-prisoners programs, right here in the Packing House.

A program of The Claremont Forum at the Claremont Packing House
17,300+ Books Mailed in 2025
500+ Letters Received Weekly
400+ Facilities in 50 States
Donate or Learn More

While many locals know the Claremont Forum for its well-curated bookstore or for organizing our beloved Sunday Farmers & Artisans Market, its most significant impact happens behind the scenes. Operating out of the historic Claremont Packing House, the Prison Library Project (PLP) has spent decades quietly championing the civil right to literacy, one package at a time.

The Scale of the Need

The statistics behind the PLP are a testament to both the massive demand for educational resources in the carceral system and the efficiency of this Claremont-based operation. In 2025 alone, the project mailed over 17,300 books to inmates in all 50 states.

This isn't just a passive donation bin; it is a high-volume logistics hub. Every week, the Forum receives 500+ letters from people in more than 400 correctional facilities across the country. These letters are often deeply personal, requesting specific tools for self-improvement that most prison libraries simply cannot provide.

Literacy as a Civil Right

For those behind bars, a book is often the only available tool for rehabilitation, vocational training, or mental escape. The PLP operates on the belief that access to information is a fundamental right. By providing GED guides, legal references, and vocational texts, the project helps incarcerated individuals prepare for a successful re-entry into society, directly addressing the roots of recidivism through education.

How Claremont Residents Can Help

The project is powered by the community, and there are direct ways you can ensure these packages continue to reach those who need them most.

Targeted Book Donations

While all quality books are appreciated, the project has a critical, ongoing need for new dictionaries and quality paperbacks. See the Donation Cheat Sheet below for exactly what they can and cannot accept.

Drop-Off Logistics

Donations are accepted daily between 10:00 AM and 5:00 PM at the Claremont Forum bookstore inside the Packing House.

Financial Support

Postage is the project's largest recurring expense. Financial donations to help cover the cost of mailing thousands of pounds of books each month are always vital.

Donation Cheat Sheet

To ensure your book donations can be accepted and safely delivered to correctional facilities, use this checklist before dropping them off. All books must be in good, usable condition.

What to Donate

  • New Dictionaries (Critical Need)
  • GED, SAT & College Prep Guides
  • Vocational & How-To Books (small business, carpentry, electrical, automotive, farming)
  • Spanish Language Books
  • Legal Reference Materials (Black's Law Dictionary, etc.)
  • Quality Paperbacks (fiction & non-fiction)

Cannot Accept

  • Hardcover Books (most facilities only allow paperbacks)
  • Spiral-Bound Books (metal coils prohibited)
  • Old or Outdated Textbooks
  • Encyclopedias
  • Magazines
  • Damaged Books (missing pages, water damage, mold, or writing)
Drop-Off Location

The Claremont Forum Bookstore

Claremont Packing House, West Atrium

Hours: Daily, 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM

Make a Day of It

Supporting the Forum is a staple of the Claremont lifestyle. A perfect local Sunday involves heading to the Claremont Farmers & Artisans Market (which the Forum also sponsors) to stock up on local produce, then walking over to the Packing House. You can drop off your book donations, browse the Forum's shelves for your own next read, and know that your support helps sustain one of the most impactful literacy programs in the United States.

How to Make the Biggest Impact

If you only do one thing, buy a new paperback dictionary and drop it off. Dictionaries are the single most-requested item from incarcerated individuals, and they wear out fast in a shared prison library. A $10 dictionary from any bookstore can change someone's ability to study for their GED, write legal documents, or simply understand the world around them.
