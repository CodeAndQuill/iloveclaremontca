More Than Just a Mall

The "Instagrammable" landmark that turned a citrus legacy into the heart of our community

Built 1922
Original Use Citrus Packing House
Restored Early 2000s
Today Local Shops & Dining

If you've spent any time in Claremont, you've likely found yourself wandering the wide, wooden-planked hallways of the Claremont Packing House. Whether you're there for a latte at Iron & Kin, a quick bite at Gus's BBQ, or to browse the shelves at The Claremont Forum, the building feels vital. But the Packing House isn't just a chic collection of shops and restaurants; it is a living monument to the industry that put Claremont on the map.

A Legacy of Citrus

1922

The College Heights Orange and Lemon Association packing house is built, becoming the busiest spot in town.

1920s–1950s

Millions of oranges are washed, graded, and packed into wooden crates right where you now sit for lunch — connecting Claremont's local groves to the rest of the world via the adjacent Santa Fe railroad.

Mid-Century

The citrus industry fades. Many of these "industrial cathedrals" across Southern California are torn down — but Claremont chooses a different path.

Early 2000s

A massive restoration preserves the building's soul, transforming it into a thriving ecosystem of local businesses.

Today

The Packing House stands as Claremont's definitive "third space" — the community hub between home and work where real connection happens.

The Rebirth: From Industrial to Artisanal

Preserving the Soul

The restoration preserved the building's most iconic features. The sawtooth roof — originally designed to let in soft, northern light for the workers — now illuminates a thriving ecosystem of small businesses.

No Big Boxes Allowed

What makes the Packing House "more than a mall" is its refusal to host big-box chains. Instead, it serves as an incubator for local talent. From the creative energy at Studio Claremont to the specialized galleries tucked into the atriums, the building prioritizes the unique over the uniform.

It's a place where you can watch an artist work, drop off books for the Prison Library Project, or find a one-of-a-kind gift that was crafted just a few feet from where you bought it.

The Ultimate Community Hub

Today, the Packing House is the definitive "third space" in Claremont — that place between home and work where community actually happens.

The Social Scene

With its open-air atriums and shared seating, it's the default meeting spot for 5C students, young families, and faculty alike.

The Events

From the bustle of the Sunday Farmers Market just outside its doors to the monthly Claremont Art Walk, there is always a reason to linger.

The "Instagram" Factor

With its exposed brick, vintage industrial scales, and iconic exterior, it's arguably the most photographed building in the Inland Empire.

Supporting the Packing House

When you shop at the Packing House, you aren't just buying a product; you're supporting the preservation of Claremont's history.

Every coffee, every meal, and every book helps keep this historic landmark breathing for the next generation.
