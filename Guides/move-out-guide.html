The 5C Move-Out Guide

How to ditch your stuff without trashing Claremont — donate, sell, recycle, repeat

Move-Out Window May 13–18, 2026
Commencement May 16–17
On-Campus Programs ReCoop · Scrapps · ReRoom

Every May, the Claremont Colleges become a controlled demolition of dorm life. Mini-fridges appear on sidewalks. Perfectly good desks end up in dumpsters. Futons are abandoned like they committed a crime. But it doesn't have to be this way. Whether you're graduating, heading home for summer, or just upgrading your setup, here is every option for getting rid of your stuff responsibly — and keeping Claremont beautiful in the process.

The Move-Out Timeline

May 6 Last day of classes
May 11–15 Finals period
May 13–17 ReCoop collection (Pomona) — 1 PM to 6 PM daily
May 16 CMC, Scripps & Pitzer commencement
May 16 (noon) Pomona non-grad move-out deadline
May 17 Pomona & Harvey Mudd commencement
May 18 Final move-out deadline (most campuses)

1. On-Campus Donation Programs

The easiest option. Each college runs its own program that collects your stuff during move-out and resells it to incoming students in the fall. Zero effort, maximum impact.

ReCoop

Pomona College

The biggest move-out program across The Claremont Colleges. ReCoop diverts over 10 tons of materials from landfills every year. Student volunteers collect donations during finals week and resell everything at deeply discounted prices to incoming freshmen in the fall.

  • When: May 13–17, 1 PM – 6 PM (staffed hours only)
  • Drop-off: Walker Lounge (North Campus) and parking lot behind Gibson Hall (South Campus)
  • Accepted: Books, clothing, shoes, blankets, furniture, electronics, lamps, storage organizers, rugs, room decor
  • Contact: sustainability@pomona.edu

Scripps Scrapps

Scripps College

Collects unwanted dorm goods at designated spots throughout residence halls. Diverts over 3 tons of waste annually. Everything gets resold at the fall sale near GJW during freshman move-in — 100% of proceeds go to student worker compensation.

  • Accepted: Mini fridges, lamps, storage organizers, small furniture, clean rugs, yoga mats, curtains, small appliances (fans, heaters, vacuums), clean kitchenware, bundled hangers
  • NOT accepted: Food, clothing, shoes, used bedding, pillows, foam toppers, hygiene products
  • Follow: @scripps_scrapps on Instagram for drop-off locations

ReRoom

Claremont McKenna College

CMC's campus sale where students can purchase gently used items from prior students at reduced cost. CMC also partners with the Institution Recycling Network to donate surplus furniture to local and overseas organizations.

Green Bike Program

Pomona & Pitzer

Don't abandon your bike. Pomona's Sustainability Office accepts bike returns at Harwood Court or the South ReCoop site during move-out. Pitzer's Green Bike Program collects abandoned bikes, refurbishes them, and redistributes them via a bi-annual raffle. They also offer free bike repairs year-round.

The Free Room

Pomona College — Year-Round

Not just a move-out thing. The Free Room in Walker is a year-round reuse room where students can donate and take gently used clothing, shoes, accessories, and supplies at no cost. Great for clearing out your closet any time of year.

2. Sell It Yourself

If your stuff has real value and you want cash for it, here are the go-to channels:

  • 7C For Sale/For Free (Facebook Group): The primary student marketplace across all seven Claremont Colleges. Over 8,500 members. Post furniture, textbooks, mini-fridges, concert tickets, apartment subleases — everything moves here during move-out season.
  • Facebook Marketplace: Cast a wider net beyond the colleges. Great for furniture and appliances that local Claremont residents would buy.
  • Claremont Colleges Off Campus Housing (Facebook Group): The place to list apartment furniture bundles for incoming students looking for off-campus housing setups.

Start listing early — the last week of April is when incoming students start shopping. By finals week, everyone is selling and no one is buying.

3. Local Donation Drop-Offs

If you missed the on-campus programs or have items they don't accept, these Claremont-area locations will take your stuff:

The Economy Shop

325 West First Street, Claremont

A volunteer-run nonprofit thrift store since 1933. Accepts clean clothing, household goods, jewelry, furniture, books, toys, small appliances, and decorative items. 98% of revenue goes back to community nonprofits.

Donation hours: Mon–Tue 9 AM–11 AM, plus Wed–Fri 10 AM–4 PM

Phone: (909) 626-7334

Goodwill — Pomona

210 East Foothill Blvd, Pomona (3 miles from campus)

The nearest full-size Goodwill. Accepts clothing, furniture, electronics, books, and household items. Open daily 10 AM–9 PM.

Phone: (909) 667-4191

Salvation Army Drop Box

1111 Mountain Ave, Claremont (at Claremont Presbyterian Church)

Clothing and small household item donations via the outdoor drop box. For large furniture pickups, call 1-800-SA-TRUCK — but call ahead to confirm availability in the Claremont area.

4. E-Waste & Recycling

Broken electronics, dead batteries, and old cables don't belong in the trash. Here's where to take them:

City of Claremont Bulky Item Pickup

If you live off campus with city trash service, you get 3 free bulky item pickups per year. This includes furniture, appliances, mattresses, and boxed e-waste. Schedule at least a week in advance.

Phone: (909) 399-5431

Connie and Dick's Automotive

A certified e-waste collection center right in Claremont. Drop off old electronics during business hours.

Address: 150 Olive Street, Claremont

Phone: (909) 626-5653

LA County Household Hazardous Waste

Free disposal events for e-waste, batteries, paint, and chemicals. Check the schedule at lacsd.org/hhw or call 1-888-CLEAN-LA.

The Golden Rule of Move-Out

Start sorting two weeks before finals — not the night before your move-out deadline. The students who wait until the last day are the ones leaving perfectly good furniture on the sidewalk because they ran out of time. List your big items on the 7C Facebook group during the last week of April, donate what doesn't sell to ReCoop or Scrapps during finals, and save the recycling run for after commencement.

Why It Matters

Every May, American colleges generate an estimated 640 million pounds of move-out waste. Programs like ReCoop and Scripps Scrapps prove that Claremont takes this seriously — but they only work when students actually use them.

When you donate your mini-fridge instead of abandoning it, an incoming freshman gets a $40 fridge instead of buying new. When you drop your books at the Economy Shop, they fund local nonprofits. When you bring your dead laptop to an e-waste center, it stays out of the landfill. It all adds up — and it keeps Claremont the kind of place worth coming back to.
