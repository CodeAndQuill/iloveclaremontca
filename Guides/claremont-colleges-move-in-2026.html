5C Move-In Dates 2026

Your game plan for the most chaotic — and exciting — week in Claremont

Moving into college is equal parts exciting and hectic. Whether you’re an incoming first-year or a returning student, navigating drop-offs, dorm setup, and traffic across the 5Cs requires a game plan — especially when late August heat in Claremont can easily top 95°F. Here’s the official breakdown of 2026 move-in dates, plus local tips from people who live here to make move-in week smooth and stress-free.

🌡️ Plan for the Foothill Heat

Late August in Claremont is notoriously hot. When loading trucks or carrying boxes up three flights of stairs in un-air-conditioned stairwells, you will feel it. A few things that make a real difference:

Pack a “First-Hour” Bag

Keep a small tote easily accessible in the front seat — not buried under boxes. Fill it with bottled water, a handheld mini fan, scissors or a utility knife, trash bags, and cleaning wipes. You won’t want to dig through five sealed boxes just to find a box cutter.

Hydrate Before You Start

Bring an insulated water bottle and fill up before you start unloading. Once you’re deep in the move, you’ll forget to drink. The Claremont heat is dry and fast — you’ll feel dehydrated before you feel thirsty.

Move in the Morning

Most colleges schedule early drop-off windows for a reason. The temperature difference between 8am and 2pm on a late-August Claremont day can be 15°F or more. Use your time slot and arrive ready to move fast.

🛍️ Don’t Buy Big Items Before You Arrive

Save room in the car (or avoid expensive oversized baggage fees at the airport) by buying bulk dorm supplies locally. Everything you need is within a short drive.

Target in Upland

The closest Target is just over the border in Upland — about a 10-minute drive east on Foothill Blvd, or reachable by Foothill Transit Line 188. Stock up on storage bins, hangers, fans, bedding, and all the dorm essentials that didn’t fit in your luggage.

Trader Joe’s

Located right on Foothill Blvd in Claremont. Great for stocking your mini-fridge on day one — snacks, grab-and-go meals, and the kind of reusable bags you’ll use for the next four years.

Check Appliance Rules First

Confirm room dimensions and appliance restrictions (mini-fridge and microwave combos, etc.) with your residential life portal before buying heavy electronics. Rules vary by college and even by dorm.

📦 Shipping & Cardboard Logistics

Mailroom Hold Times

Campus mailrooms across the 5Cs get flooded during move-in week. If you ship boxes ahead of time, double-check your campus postal address format precisely — each college has a specific format and errors cause real delays.

Break Down Boxes Immediately

Every dorm courtyard will have designated recycling zones during move-in week. Flattening your boxes the moment they’re empty keeps hallways clear, fire-safe, and less overwhelming for the families still trying to get in behind you.

🍔 Grab a Move-In Lunch in the Village

When you need a break from the heat and chaos, head straight down into the Claremont Village — it’s a short walk or drive from all five campuses. Quick sandwiches, tacos, and cold drinks are all within reach. Check our Restaurant Guide for the full rundown, or if the parents are in town with the credit card, our Dining Guide covers the nicer sit-down spots worth making a reservation for.

Coffee & Cold Brew

Need an afternoon caffeine boost? The Village has several great coffee shops with shaded patios and iced drinks. Our Off-Campus Coffee Guide covers every option near campus.

Move-In Week Pro Tips

Arrive at your drop-off window start time — not 15 minutes early, not 30 minutes late. The colleges coordinate traffic carefully during these windows and arriving outside them creates real backups for other families.

One car, one trip — if you’re flying in and shipping boxes, resist the urge to also rent a car full of stuff. Use the Target run on day two when you know exactly what the room is missing.

Say hi to your neighbors — move-in is the easiest day to introduce yourself. Everyone is equally frazzled and equally excited. The awkward knock on the door gets easier when you’re all lugging boxes at the same time.
