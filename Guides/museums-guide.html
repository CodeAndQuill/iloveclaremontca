Claremont's Museums & Cultural Collections

A local guide to eight world-class collections hiding in plain sight in the 91711.

Whether you're looking for world-class contemporary art, ancient fossils, or rare global artifacts, Claremont punches far above its weight when it comes to cultural institutions. Between the town's deep history and the academic energy of The Claremont Colleges, you can spend an entire weekend exploring serious museum collections without leaving town.

A quick note for anyone who Googled their way here: if the Petersen Automotive Museum showed up in your local search, that's a separate institution on Wilshire Boulevard in Los Angeles — about 40 miles west of here. This guide is dedicated to the cultural collections that actually live in Claremont.

New exhibitions, free-day reminders, and after-hours art walks — one email, only when there's something worth showing up for.

On the Campuses

Three of Claremont's museums sit on the Claremont Colleges — clustered along College Avenue, all walkable from each other, and all free to the public.

Benton Museum of Art at Pomona College

A striking contemporary building by Boston-based architect Machado Silvetti, the Benton sits at the eastern edge of the Pomona College campus and serves as a quiet gateway between the town and the colleges. The permanent collection runs from Renaissance prints to contemporary work, paired with a celebrated rotating exhibition program.

  • Free for everyone: General admission to exhibitions and events is free and open to the public year-round.
  • The Project Series: A longstanding rotating program that brings ambitious contemporary work — often from Southern California artists — into focused, scholarly installations.
  • The courtyard: The central open-air courtyard is one of the most underrated quiet spaces in the city. Bring a book.
Where: 120 W. Bonita Avenue, Claremont (at College Avenue) · Admission: Free · Check site for current hours

Ruth Chandler Williamson Gallery (Scripps College)

Scripps's small-but-serious art gallery, anchored by the Samella Lewis Contemporary Art Collection and known for ambitious rotating shows that pull from the Claremont Colleges' deep art holdings. A favorite of locals who know to time their visits around openings.

  • Samella Lewis Contemporary Art Collection: A foundational collection of work by African American and African diaspora artists, kept on rotating view alongside the exhibition calendar.
  • Senior shows and traveling exhibitions: Programming runs the gamut — student senior shows, traveling national exhibitions, recent acquisitions, and curated themes.
  • Check before you go: Hours are tied to active exhibitions; the gallery sometimes closes between shows.
Where: 251 East 11th Street, Claremont · When: Wed–Sun 12–5 PM during exhibitions

Pitzer College Art Galleries

Pitzer's contemporary art galleries punch well above their size — focused on ambitious, often socially-engaged exhibitions across multiple gallery spaces on the Pitzer campus. The programming leans into emerging and experimental work you won't see at a more traditional museum.

  • Contemporary focus: Rotating exhibitions of contemporary work, with regular emphasis on socially engaged and environmental art.
  • Multiple spaces: Programming runs across several gallery spaces on the Pitzer campus.
  • Easy drop-in: The compressed Wed–Sat afternoon window makes it a natural stop on a lunchtime campus walk.
Where: 1050 N. Mills Avenue, Claremont · When: Wed–Sat 1–4 PM

In the Village & Memorial Park

Three more cultural collections sit within easy walking distance of one another — the Lewis at the Depot, the Folk Music Center on Yale Avenue, and Claremont Heritage at Garner House in Memorial Park. Add a coffee stop and you have a half-day.

Claremont Lewis Museum of Art

Housed inside the lovingly restored 1927 Claremont Depot, this community-rooted museum tells the story of art-makers from the foothills of the San Gabriel Valley. It's the anchor of Claremont's local art community and an easy first stop for anyone arriving by Metrolink.

  • The location: Right beside the Metrolink platform at the south end of the Village — an easy walk from First Street, Yale Avenue shops, and the Packing House.
  • Free days: The museum offers regular free admission days throughout the week — check the museum site for the current schedule before you visit.
  • First Saturday Art Walk: Extends hours into the evening on the first Saturday of each month for receptions and community programming alongside the Village's other galleries.
Where: At the historic Claremont Depot, south Village · Confirm current hours and admission on the museum site before visiting

Folk Music Center

Technically a music store, functionally a living folk-instrument museum. Owned by the family of Grammy-winning songwriter Ben Harper (his mother Ellen Harper still runs the shop he grew up inside), the walls are lined with hundreds of rare, antique, and handcrafted instruments from around the world.

  • Living museum: Hundreds of rare and antique instruments and artifacts from cultures around the world — many of them accessible to handle and hear (with care, and a chat with the staff first).
  • Lessons for all ages: Professional music lessons for beginners through advanced players across a wide range of instruments.
  • Famous family: Founded by Ben Harper's grandparents Charles and Dorothy Chase in 1958, and still run by the family today.
Where: 220 Yale Avenue, Claremont (the Village) · When: Tue 12–5 PM, Wed–Sat 10 AM–5 PM (closed Sun & Mon)

Claremont Heritage & the Ginger Elliott Exhibition Center

A local-history nonprofit headquartered in the historic Garner House at Memorial Park. Their Ginger Elliott Exhibition Center hosts rotating shows — recent runs have included plein air landscapes and Route 66 photography — alongside walking tours, archives, and a long-running speaker series. The best place to learn what Claremont actually was before it became what it is now.

  • Ginger Elliott Gallery: Rotating exhibitions of regional art and local history in a dedicated gallery directly behind the Garner House.
  • Walking tours: Periodic guided tours of historic Claremont neighborhoods — check the events calendar.
  • Archives access: A working historical archive of Claremont newspapers, photos, and ephemera, available for research.
Where: Garner House, Memorial Park — 840 N. Indian Hill Boulevard, Claremont · Office: Mon–Fri 10 AM–3 PM (call ahead for gallery viewing outside event hours)

Off the Beaten Path

Two more museums sit further from the Village — both worth the trip, both with their own quirks of access. Plan your visit around their hours.

Raymond M. Alf Museum of Paleontology

Tucked away on the campus of The Webb Schools in the foothills north of town, this is one of the few accredited natural history museums in the country located on a high school campus. The collection spans billions of years — from trilobites to dinosaurs — and is especially famous for its fossilized animal tracks. Dinosaur-loving kids and serious researchers both leave happy.

  • Hall of Life: Walks visitors through billions of years of Earth's history, from early invertebrates through the age of dinosaurs.
  • Hall of Footprints: Globally significant collection of fossilized tracks — follow the footprints of extinct camels, elephants, and more.
  • Heads-up: Because it sits on a private school campus, the visitor entrance is off Baseline Road — allow a few extra minutes to check in.
Where: 1175 W. Baseline Road, Claremont · When: Wed–Fri 11 AM–5 PM, Sat 10 AM–5 PM (check site for school-year variations and admission)

Petterson Museum of Intercultural Art

Nestled inside the Pilgrim Place retirement community, this small, deeply curated museum offers a quiet window into global material culture. It preserves an eclectic collection of textiles, folk art, ceremonial pieces, and historic artifacts gathered by Pilgrim Place residents over decades of work around the world.

  • The collection: Artifacts and folk art from Asia, Africa, the Americas, and beyond — thousands of pieces rotated through a compact gallery.
  • The vibe: Reflective, quiet, and intentionally small in scale. A wonderful counterpoint to the larger campus museums.
  • Plan ahead: The museum keeps limited public hours (typically Saturday mornings) — confirm the current schedule with Pilgrim Place before you go.
Where: Pilgrim Place — 625 Mayflower Road, Claremont · Hours: Limited; check Pilgrim Place for current schedule
How to build a Claremont museum day

The Village & Memorial Park loop: Pair the Lewis Museum (at the Depot) with the Folk Music Center (Yale Avenue, three blocks north) and Claremont Heritage at Garner House (a short walk into Memorial Park). Half a day on foot, plus coffee and lunch in the Village.

The campus loop: The Benton (Pomona), Williamson Gallery (Scripps), and Pitzer Galleries are all within a 15-minute walk of each other along College Avenue. Add a slow wander through the Campus Walk — the architecture itself is part of the experience. See our free things to do guide for the campus walking route.

The off-the-beaten-path day: The Alf (Webb Schools, north of town) and the Petterson (Pilgrim Place) are the two smaller, harder-to-reach collections — both worth the trip, but check hours carefully.

First Saturday of the month: The Village turns into an art walk from 6–9 PM — galleries open late, the Lewis hosts receptions, and the Folk Music Center often joins in. It's the single best night to museum-hop on foot.
